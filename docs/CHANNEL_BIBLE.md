# Channel Bible

Approved editorial direction for topic selection, scripting, review, and publishing.

## Mission

Tell short, hand-drawn stories that help viewers discover and understand overlooked history.

## Target audience

Curious English-speaking adults with no specialist historical knowledge. Each video must be understandable without prior context.

## Content pillars

1. Forgotten people
2. Hidden origins of familiar things
3. Overlooked events that changed history

## Format

- Typical duration: 3–5 minutes
- Publishing cadence: One video every two weeks
- Story structure: Hook → context → explanation/story → payoff → close

## Voice and narrator

- Tone: Warm, curious, and conversational
- Narrator personality: A thoughtful guide, not a lecturer
- Humor level: Light and occasional; never trivialize people or suffering
- Vocabulary/reading level: Plain English for adults with no specialist knowledge; briefly explain necessary historical terms and avoid academic jargon

## Boundaries

- Topics we cover: Well-sourced history across cultures and periods that fits at least one content pillar
- Topics we avoid: Pseudohistory, unsupported conspiracy claims, gratuitous violence, partisan present-day framing, exploitative treatment of tragedy, and topics without reliable sources
- Claims requiring extra review: medical, legal, financial, safety, active conflicts, and breaking news
- Never sacrifice factual accuracy for a cleaner story without clearly labeling interpretation.

## Success criteria

For the first three episodes:

- Publish on the biweekly cadence.
- Trace every factual claim to research and pass human review.
- Reach at least 50% average percentage viewed, or document the main retention lesson.
- Capture meaningful audience feedback when available.
- Record one evidence-backed production improvement after each episode.
